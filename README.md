# The Deductible as a Tax on the Sick

Liquidity, Myopia, and the Case for Mandatory Pre-Deductible Coverage of High-Value Chronic Care

Omar Hassan · Barcelona School of Economics · June 2026

Solo-authored policy paper for Topics in Public Economics (course grade 8.0/10).

## Question

High-deductible health plans charge patients the full price of care until the deductible is met, so the price a chronically ill patient faces drops sharply during the year. Forward-looking patients whose spending will clearly pass the deductible should ignore this and keep using essential care at a steady rate. The paper asks what happens instead, and whether high-value chronic care such as insulin should be covered before the deductible.

## Findings

- In 2018 Medical Expenditure Panel Survey data, diabetics in high-deductible plans paid about 57% more out of pocket per year than diabetics in other private plans ($2,150 against $1,370), although total spending was similar (about $14,800 against $15,500).
- The mean out-of-pocket cost of an insulin fill fell from about $65 in the first quarter to $33 in the fourth, the pattern a deductible produces.
- Using Chetty's (2008) sufficient-statistic framework, the paper separates liquidity and myopia frictions from moral hazard and argues for mandatory first-dollar coverage of high-value chronic care.

These comparisons are descriptive. The paper proposes two designs to estimate the causal effect, an employer-level cluster-randomised trial and a triple-difference design around IRS Notice 2019-45; neither has been run.
